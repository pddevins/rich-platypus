I know considerably more about myself than I did twelve months ago, and almost
none of it was learned on purpose. That's how it goes, apparently. You don't get to
choose the curriculum, only whether you show up for it.

<!--more-->

Last year took a number of things I had treated as permanent fixtures and revealed
them to be arrangements — including the one I'd assumed was the least negotiable of
all. A household reorganises itself around a fact like that whether or not anyone in
it is ready, and mine did. What survived got tested hard enough that I now know
which parts of my life were load-bearing and which were habit, and I could not have
told you the difference in January.

The house has different rhythms now. There are days it's full and loud and there's
someone asking me to find a specific sock, and there are days it's quiet in a way I
had no practice with. Getting good at the second kind took most of the year. I was
not good at it in the spring. I'm decent at it now, which surprises me more than
almost anything else on the list.

That's the part I'd want to tell someone in the middle of a hard stretch, if they
asked. The distinction between solitude and loneliness isn't semantic and it isn't
automatic. They feel identical for a while. Then at some point — and I can't tell
you what triggers it — an evening alone stops registering as an absence and starts
registering as an evening. Nothing changed except that I stopped waiting for it to
be over.

What I found in there was a version of myself I'd shelved. Not a better one,
necessarily. An earlier one. Someone with more curiosity than schedule, who used to
start things without a clear reason and was fine abandoning half of them. That
person had been slowly optimized away over a decade of being extremely responsible,
and it turns out he was still in there, waiting for enough unstructured hours to
resurface.

I want to be careful not to make this sound like a straightforward win, because it
wasn't and the honest version matters more than the tidy one. There were months in
there I would not repeat. Some of what changed, I'd change back. Grief for a
particular shape of life is not a phase you complete, and anyone who tells you it
resolves on a schedule is selling something. What I'll say is that it becomes
something you carry rather than something that carries you, and the difference in
weight is enormous even when the mass is the same.

The reason I'm writing on the first of January rather than the thirty-first of
December is that I'm more interested in the direction than the accounting. I've
done enough looking back. The genuinely surprising thing about arriving at a fresh
start you didn't request is how much of it is actually free — not the circumstances,
which are what they are, but the decisions. There's no accumulated inertia telling
me how this year has to go. I get to pick.

So I'm picking curiosity, mostly. There are things I want to build and things I
want to learn to do badly before I do them well, and for the first time in a long
while there's room in the calendar for both. I want to be someone my kids watch
being interested in things, which is a better inheritance than being someone they
watch being busy.

None of that requires the year to have been good. It just requires me to still be
here and paying attention, and I am, and I am.
